## Money Stuff — the Avis short squeeze

**[Read on Bloomberg ↗](https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/newsletters/2026-04-29/short-swing-short-squeeze)** · Matt Levine, _Money Stuff_

### The price action

Avis went from **$99 on March 20** to **$713 on April 21** and crashed back to
**$182**. The squeeze itself is not the interesting part — the ownership
structure that produced it is.

### The position

Pentwater and SRS held **69% of the stock** outright, but **108% of the
economic ownership** counting cash-settled total return swaps. SRS held a
board seat, which put them in earnings blackout. Pentwater spread their
exposure across multiple funds — which is what made the unwind legally
interesting.

### Mechanics, briefly

A short seller borrows shares from a prime broker, sells them for cash,
and posts collateral that gets marked to market. If the price rises, the
short faces margin calls. To close, they buy back in the market and return
the shares.

A squeeze self-reinforces through three loops:

- **Margin requirements rise** — more collateral, less optionality.
- **Recall risk** — the original lender can recall anytime; if the short
  can't borrow replacements, they're forced to buy in.
- **Borrow costs spike** — from 0.5–2% to 200% annualized.

### Cash-settled total return swaps

The instrument doing the heavy lifting. One party gets all upside and
downside; the other gets capital priced off LIBOR/SOFR. **No shares change
hands.** Banks buy the underlying for their own hedging, so the position
sits on **bank** balance sheets, not the fund's.

This is the structure Bill Hwang used at Archegos — nothing showed up on
balance sheets until the 2021 margin call. The SEC tightened swap-
disclosure rules in 2023; it didn't close the gap.

### Section 16(b) and "lowest-in, highest-out"

The 1943 _Smolowe v. Delendo_ rule still governs short-swing matching for
anyone with >10% beneficial ownership trading within six months: statutory
profit is calculated by matching the **lowest buy** against the **highest
sell**, ignoring economic reality.

Levine's worked example: buy 100 shares each at $50, $60, $70, $80, $90,
$100, $110, $120, $130, $140 and sell 100 shares at the same prices.
Actual P&L is zero. Matchable "profit" under 16(b): $50 against $140 =
$90/share, then $60 against $130, and so on. **Statutory disgorgement can
be enormous even when real P&L is flat or negative.**

Pentwater dodged the matching on a parallel fund and **sold 4.3M shares
for ~$1.75B in proceeds**. Avis (the issuer) got a windfall from the 16(b)
disgorgement on the directly-held bucket — no dilution to existing
shareholders.

### The arb idea

The structural setup hiding in plain sight:

- Find names with **short interest > 30%**.
- Build to **>50% holder ownership**.
- Creep up against the **Section 16 disclosure threshold**.
- Insiders can't sell into the squeeze; the mispricing is mechanical.

Levine doesn't quite endorse this as a strategy — but he doesn't argue it
won't work either.

## SemiAnalysis — AI value capture is migrating to the model labs

**[Read on SemiAnalysis ↗](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/ai-value-capture-the-shift-to-model)** · May 1, 2026

The piece is long; this is what I'm taking forward.

### The migration (2023–25 → 2026)

```
        2023–2025                        2026 (NOW)
   ┌────────────────┐              ┌────────────────┐
   │ INFRASTRUCTURE │              │   MODEL LABS   │
   │  CAPTURES ALL  │    ══════>   │  CAPTURE ALL   │
   │   THE VALUE    │              │   THE VALUE    │
   └────────────────┘              └────────────────┘
           │                                │
           ▼                                ▼
   • Nvidia (+25% AH May'23)       • Anthropic ARR:
   • Vistra +265% (2024)             $9B → $44B+
   • GE Vernova +146% (2024)       • Inference margins:
   • SanDisk/WDC/Seagate/            38% → 70%+
     Micron all +200% (2025)
```

Through 2025 the value flowed to **picks-and-shovels**:

- Nvidia (the AH +25% print after May '23 was the kickoff).
- Memory cycle: SanDisk, WDC, Seagate, Micron all up **>200%** in 2025.
- Power names: Vistra **+265%**, GE Vernova **+146%** in 2024.

2026 is the inversion. **Anthropic ARR went from $9B to $44B+. Inference
gross margins went from 38% to 70%+.** The labs that were "famously bad"
on margins are now the value capture layer.

### Why each token is worth more

The agentic inflection (late 2025) means tasks that used to cost
**thousands of dollars and tens of person-hours** now finish in **minutes
for a few dollars in tokens**. SemiAnalysis discloses its own internal
spend: **$10.95M annual run-rate on Claude tokens**, ~30% of employee
comp, **~5B tokens per employee per month** — 5x what Meta reports.
Power-law usage: top users above 100B tokens/month.

### The Opus pricing paradox

```
STICKER PRICE              TRUE BLENDED PRICE
$5 / $25 per MTok    →     ~$0.99 per MTok
(input / output)           on agentic tasks
        ▲
        │
   WHY? Agentic workloads have:
   • Input:Output ratio of 300:1 (Claude Code)
   • Cache hit rates of 90%+
   • Cached tokens cost only $0.50/MTok
   • Most tokens land in cheapest tier
```

Sticker is **$5 / $25 per MTok** input/output. **Blended price on real
agentic workloads is ~$0.99/MTok.** Three reasons:

- Input:output ratio of **300:1** on Claude Code.
- Cache hit rates **90%+**.
- Cached tokens at **$0.50/MTok** — the cheapest tier dominates.

The headline price scares no one who actually uses it at scale.

### Throughput explosion

B300 running DeepSeek R1 (8K in → 1K out):

```
No optimizations:             ████  ~1K tok/sec/GPU

+ wideEP + disagg:            ████████████████████  ~8K tok/sec/GPU

+ wideEP + disagg + MTP:      ████████████████████████████████  ~14K
                               ↑ 14x just from SOFTWARE

ADD HARDWARE:
GB300 NVL72 vs H100 (FP8):  17x throughput
GB300 NVL72 vs H100 (FP4):  32x throughput
GB300 NVL72 TCO:             only ~70% higher than H100
```

Hardware on top: **GB300 NVL72 vs H100 — 17x at FP8, 32x at FP4.** Total
cost of ownership only ~70% higher than H100. The cost-per-useful-FLOP
collapse is the real story.

### Why lab margins won't get competed away (yet)

Two moats:

1. **Real frontier quality gap.** Open source — Kimi K2.6 at $0.95/$4 per
   MTok — barely dents Opus pricing. Benchmarks lie; production knowledge
   work needs closed models.
2. **Compute scarcity.** Demand >> supply for the foreseeable future.
   Anthropic is already gating Claude Code behind $100+/mo tiers and
   blocking third-party harnesses.

### The pricing power paradox

**Who raised prices**: memory (6x in a year), neocloud H100 rentals (+40%
off the Oct 2025 lows), inference providers (Fireworks, Baseten, Fal).

**Who didn't**: Nvidia and TSMC — the two with the most pricing power.

Nvidia's restraint comes from three places: antitrust scrutiny is already
heavy across GPUs/interconnect/software; an old framework that assumed
willingness-to-pay-per-FLOP declines over time (it's now compounding);
and the TSMC playbook — full utilization, no scarcity pricing, prioritize
long-term relationships. Jensen literally said in 2024: _"TSMC should
charge more."_

### SOCAMM2 — the hidden pricing lever

GB300 used **soldered LPDDR5X**, bundled into the system at ~75% GM. VR
NVL72 / Rubin moves to **SOCAMM2** — socketed, modular, repriceable
memory as a separate line item.

- 1Q26 SOCAMM cost: ~$8/GB.
- Exit 2026: ~$13/GB. Reasonable midpoint: ~$10/GB.
- Mobile LPDDR5X reference: $6–7/GB.
- Target gross margin: **~60%**.

Memory becomes the cartridge; Nvidia becomes the printer. Customers will
accept it because (a) supply is locked up via Nvidia LTAs, (b) VR NVL72
has no real alternative, and (c) Nvidia is eating an input price hike
itself.

### The capex/W smoking gun

|          | GB300    | VR NVL72         |
| -------- | -------- | ---------------- |
| Capex/W  | $37.4/W  | $38.1/W (≈ flat) |
| TDP/chip | 1400W    | 2300W (+64%)     |
| Perf/W   | baseline | >2x improvement  |

Capex/W has historically risen generation over generation as perf/W
gains let vendors capture more of the value created. Going flat is
**under-pricing relative to value delivered**. This is the line in the
piece I keep going back to.

### Cost floor vs value ceiling

```
┌─────────────────────────┐   ┌─────────────────────────┐
│   COST-BASED (FLOOR)    │   │  VALUE-BASED (CEILING)  │
├─────────────────────────┤   ├─────────────────────────┤
│ GB300 today:            │   │ Anchor: GB300 5-yr      │
│ • 15.6% IRR over 5yr    │   │ rental ~$0.70/PFLOP     │
│ • 15% prepay            │   │                         │
│                         │   │ Implies VR NVL72        │
│ VR NVL72 needs:         │   │ ceiling at parity:      │
│ ≥ $4.92/GPU/hr          │   │ ~$12.25/GPU/hr          │
└─────────────────────────┘   └─────────────────────────┘
                  └──────────────────┘
            HUGE GAP — unprecedented vs prior gens
            Today: $0.28/PFLOP (60% below trend)
```

For VR NVL72:

- **Floor (cost-based):** ~$4.92/GPU/hr to hit a 15.6% IRR over 5 years
  with 15% prepay.
- **Ceiling (value-based, anchored to GB300 $0.70/PFLOP rentals at parity):**
  ~$12.25/GPU/hr.

The gap between floor and ceiling is unprecedented vs prior generations.
Today's pricing is ~$0.28/PFLOP — a **60% drop vs GB300 NVL72** and
**below trend improvement**. A ~40% Nvidia price hike would still leave
below-trend cost improvements for customers.

### Where the value flows today

```
END USERS
($1000s of human work → $ in tokens)
        │  pays for tokens
        ▼
AI LABS ◄══════════════ CAPTURING MOST VALUE NOW
(Anthropic 70%+ margins)
        │  rents compute
        ▼
NEOCLOUDS / HYPERSCALERS ◄══ some value capture
(inference providers: +40% from Oct '25 lows)
        │  buys hardware
        ▼
┌───────────────────────────────────────┐
│            HARDWARE STACK             │
│  ┌──────────────┐  ┌───────────────┐  │
│  │    NVIDIA    │  │ MEMORY (+6x)  │  │
│  │  (UNDER-     │  │ ── repriced   │  │
│  │   PRICED)    │  │   already     │  │
│  └──────────────┘  └───────────────┘  │
│  ┌──────────────┐                     │
│  │     TSMC     │  (UNDER-PRICED)     │
│  └──────────────┘                     │
└───────────────────────────────────────┘
```

End users pay tokens → **AI labs at 70%+ margins** → neocloud/hyperscaler
rentals (rising) → hardware stack where **Nvidia and TSMC are
deliberately under-priced** relative to value, and memory is the one
hardware line that's already repriced.

### The inevitable transition

As inference ROI gets more legible, scrutiny on pricing decreases, GPU
infra shifts from cost-based to value-based pricing, and Nvidia gets
political cover to follow. SOCAMM2 is the cleanest mechanism — it
reprices on its own cadence without touching headline GPU pricing.

### The line I'm keeping

> Demand for AI compute is compounding. Supply is structurally
> constrained. Yet the bottleneck holders are pricing like it's still 2024.

The pie is still growing. Nobody has to lose for Nvidia to start charging
what the labs are happily paying.
