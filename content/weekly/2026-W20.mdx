    notes: "Long technical primer on the Electronic Design Automation industry — the toolchain that turns chip designs into silicon. Part 1 of a 3-part series. The 13-stage waterfall: Planning → Architecture → RTL → RTL Verification → RTL Freeze → FW/SW Dev → Physical Design → Signoff → Tapeout → Fabrication & Packaging → Post-Silicon Validation → System Integration → Production. AMD MI455X used as the example: 320B transistors, 12 logic dies on 2nm/3nm, hybrid-bonding 3D stack, HBM4, 224G SerDes. Big Three EDA: Synopsys (Design Compiler, VCS, IC Compiler II, PrimeTime, ZeBu, Sentaurus), Cadence (Innovus, Xcelium, JasperGold, Palladium, Tempus), Siemens EDA (Questa, Calibre, ex-Mentor; acquired 2017 for $4.5B). Verification is 70% of total project effort. 20–30% of a modern SoC's RTL is custom; the rest is licensed IP. PDK access tiers: Apple/AMD/Nvidia = JDA Tier 1 (3+ years pre-prod); EDA/IP vendors Tier 2; standard customers Tier 3; academia Tier 4. Intel 18A PDK timeline: 0.3 (Sep 2022) → 0.5 (Mar 2023) → 0.9 (Sep 2023) → 1.0 (Jul 2024) → Panther Lake launch (Jan 2026). TSMC FinFlex / NanoFlex: mixing standard-cell heights for PPA; Apple uses 3-2 on high-perf cores, 2-1 on the rest. DTCO links the whole tool stack; STCO extends to package and chiplets. Intel's Ponte Vecchio (47 dies, 5 nodes, EMIB + Foveros) is the cautionary tale; AMD's MI455X is the target."

## Money Stuff — KKR Buys Back Some Private Credit

**[Read on Bloomberg ↗](https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-05-11/kkr-buys-back-some-private-credit)** · _Money Stuff_, Matt Levine, May 11

### FS KKR — preferred-at-NAV + tender-at-discount

The core puzzle for a BDC manager: a Business Development Company is a
retail-facing private credit fund. It marks its loan portfolio to a NAV (say
$20/share), but the market values it at $15. Two opposite impulses:

1. **Buy at NAV** ($20) to signal "we really believe in our marks."
2. **Buy at the market discount** ($15) because that's a better trade.

KKR's actual move on FS KKR Capital Corp (FSK), per the earnings release:

```
$300M KKR INJECTION INTO FSK
══════════════════════════════════════════════════════
$150M  PREFERRED EQUITY              $150M  TENDER OFFER
       │                                    │
       ├─ convertible at $18.83             ├─ price: $11.00 / share
       │   = Q1 NAV per share               │   (vs $10.84 last close)
       │                                    │
       ├─ pays 5% cash + 7% PIK             ├─ "good deal" move
       │                                    │
       └─ "vote of confidence" move         └─ buys at the discount

       ─────────────────────────────────────────
                       synthesis:
       do BOTH impulses, half the capital on each
```

The preferred conversion at NAV is the confidence move; the $11 tender is
the trade. Doing both = synthesising the two impulses.

## SemiAnalysis — The EDA Primer: From RTL to Silicon (Part 1 of 3)

**[Read on SemiAnalysis ↗](https://semianalysis.com/2026/05/12/the-eda-primer-from-rtl-to-silicon-part-1/)** · May 12

Long technical primer on the Electronic Design Automation industry — the toolchain that turns chip designs into silicon. The 13-stage waterfall is the spine of the piece:

```
SEMICONDUCTOR DESIGN WATERFALL (13 stages)
═════════════════════════════════════════════
 1. Planning              ← PPACt goals
 2. Architecture          ← block diagram, NoC, DSE
 3. RTL Design            ← SystemVerilog, IP integration
 4. RTL Verification      ← UVM testbench OR Formal (JasperGold, VC Formal)
 5. RTL Freeze            ← coverage closure → no more functional changes
 6. FW/SW Development     ← parallel; ZeBu, Palladium emulators
 7. Physical Design       ← Synthesis → Place → Route → CTS → DFT
 8. Signoff               ← DRC, LVS, ERC, STA, Power
 9. Tapeout               ← GDSII to foundry, OPC/SRAFs added
10. Fabrication & Pkg     ← 8-12 wks; chiplets, CoWoS
11. Post-Silicon Validation ← ATE, burn-in, steppings (A0, B1, E5...)
12. System Integration    ← reference boards, RVP
13. Production            ← yield optimization, FA loop
```

### Key takeaways from Part 1

- **The crunch.** Chip complexity up ~50%/year; design productivity only ~20%/year. One-third of the US semiconductor workforce is over 55.
- **AMD MI455X.** 320B transistors, 12 logic dies on 2nm/3nm, hybrid-bonding 3D stacking, HBM4, 224G SerDes. Each respin = tens of millions for a new mask set + months lost.
- **Big Three EDA.**
  - **Synopsys** — Design Compiler (synthesis, 1987), VCS, IC Compiler II, PrimeTime, ZeBu, Sentaurus.
  - **Cadence** — Innovus (P&R), Xcelium, JasperGold, Palladium, Tempus.
  - **Siemens EDA** (ex-Mentor Graphics, acquired 2017 for $4.5B) — Questa, Calibre, deep verification.
- **Verification = 70% of total project effort.** Verification engineers are the fastest-growing job category in chip design.
- **About 20–30% of a modern SoC's RTL is custom**; the rest is licensed IP (ARM cores, Synopsys DesignWare for PCIe/USB/DDR).

### PDK access tiers

```
PDK ACCESS TIERS
═══════════════════════════════════════════
Tier 1   Apple / AMD / Nvidia        3+ years pre-production
Tier 2   EDA & IP vendors            ~1-2 years pre-production
Tier 3   Standard foundry customers  at production release
Tier 4   Academia / research         lagged + sandbox versions
```

### Intel 18A PDK timeline

```
0.3  Sep 2022      Early bring-up
0.5  Mar 2023      Architecture-ready
0.9  Sep 2023      Near-final rules
1.0  Jul 2024      Production-ready
1.0  Jan 2026      Panther Lake launches
```

### TSMC FinFlex / NanoFlex

Mixing standard cells of different heights for PPA. Apple uses **3-2 FinFlex** on high-performance cores and denser **2-1** on the rest.

### DTCO vs STCO

- **DTCO** (Design-Tech Co-Optimization) — link the whole tool stack into one feedback loop.
- **STCO** (System-Tech Co-Optimization) — extends to system + package: chiplet partitioning, interposers.
- Intel's **Ponte Vecchio** (47 dies, 5 nodes, EMIB + Foveros) is the cautionary tale.
- AMD's **MI455X** is the target — 12 dies, two nodes, hybrid bonding.

Part 2 covers EDA business and market analysis + the Chinese EDA effort. Part 3 covers AI disruption of EDA itself.

## Money Stuff — Nobody Can Read the Prospectus

**[Read on Bloomberg ↗](https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/articles/2026-05-13/nobody-can-read-the-prospectus)** · _Money Stuff_, Matt Levine, May 13

### CSG / Hunterbrook — white-on-white prospectus disclosure

**Czechoslovak Group (CSG)** — Czech arms manufacturer — went public on Euronext Amsterdam in January at €25/share, traded to €33, then dropped almost 50% since March.

**Hunterbrook** — the hedge fund that is also a newspaper — alleges CSG omitted material from its IPO prospectus. The specific omission: **Petr Kratochvíl**, a minority shareholder with "extraordinary" rights over CSG's most important subsidiary, exercised a put option days before the IPO demanding **€1.4 billion**.

The disclosure trick (per Seznam Zprávy, Czech outlet):

```
THE WHITE-ON-WHITE PROSPECTUS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
March 2026 annual report
   │
   ├─ Section on Kratochvíl put-exercise: PRESENT
   │     │
   │     └─ rendered in white text on a white background
   │           │
   │           ├─► visible to:    machines / LLMs / parsers
   │           └─► invisible to:  humans reading the document
   │
CSG's defence: software glitch (supplier Dynamo Design)
```

Levine's flip — the deeper point:

```
2010s        Hide bad news in dense legalese, page 73
2020s        LLMs read everything → that defence breaks
2026         Hide bad news in WHITE-ON-WHITE → only machines see it
              (the "this candidate is exceptional" trick from
               GPT-screening-of-CVs, applied to corporate filings)

near future  All disclosures read EXCLUSIVELY by computers
              → the new trick is hiding from the MACHINES, harder.
```

### Skechers — appraisal arbitrage as a volatility trade

**Mechanics.** When a public company is acquired for cash, dissenters can sue in Delaware for "fair value." Buy stock just before close → sue → court-determined value + statutory interest.

Historically the trade had a **bond-with-call-option shape**:

```
APPRAISAL PAYOUT SHAPE (historical)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════
At minimum    deal price + statutory interest
On upside     Delaware court awards > deal price
On downside   (modern) court sometimes awards < deal price
              — more symmetric risk than the old asymmetric one
```

Higher volatility helps in two ways: (a) wider plausible "fair value" range, more upside in court; (b) **vol creates cheap LBOs** because PE firms hunt when public markets crater.

**The Skechers case** (the marquee appraisal action right now):

```
SKECHERS — APPRAISAL TIMELINE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Jan 2025    Stock at $78.24
Apr 2 2025  Trump tariff announcement → China/Vietnam shoe
            makers crushed
May 5 2025  3G Capital announces buyout @ $63/share
              ↳ 30% premium … to the 15-day VWAP
                AFTER the tariff crash
Sep 2025    Deal closes for $9.4B
            Greenberg family (60% voting power) approved
            without a minority shareholder vote
2026        Funds owning $1.3B challenge
            Settlement crept: $63 → $64 → $65 / share
```

**Causal chain:** US tariff policy → public-market volatility → cheap LBOs → appraisal cases. **Policy creates the trade.**

### Segantii / BofA / Esprit — pre-hedging vs front-running

Dealers are _"in the moving business, not the storage business."_ A common workflow:

1. Customer says "I might want to sell $100M of XYZ"
2. Dealer **pre-sells** some of it by calling other customers
3. Then buys from the original customer

Same workflow → two names depending on context:

```
FRONT-RUNNING                  PRE-HEDGING
(illegal — using MNPI          (legal in some venues —
 about a customer order)        FX / futures / options,
                                particularly at-the-close orders)
```

**The HK insider trading trial** alleges this looked like the illegal flavour:

```
ESPRIT BLOCK — THE ALLEGATION
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Lone Pine owned 195.6M Esprit shares.

BofA Merrill in EXPLORATORY exercise (no mandate yet)
   │
   ▼
Merrill calls SEGANTII (Simon Sadler, "Asia's block-trade king")
   │ Segantii already owns Esprit
   ▼
Segantii sells its existing Esprit and goes SHORT
   at HK$5.20 – 5.35
   │
   ▼ hours later
Lone Pine gives BofA an order to sell at HK$4.68
   (10%+ lower)
   │
   ▼
BofA stuck with 27% of shares on its balance sheet.
```

HK prosecutors: illegal tipping + insider trading. Merrill: "exploratory." The legal/illegal line turns on whether the deal was mandated and whether Segantii was a counterparty or a tipped trader.

### Live trading esports — Boredom Markets Hypothesis 2.0

Levine's old theory: **prices of speculative assets vary inversely with how many other fun things there are to do.** Covid → retail trading boom. Post-Covid we got entertainment back but trading didn't collapse — partly because **trading itself got more entertaining**.

The new frontier (Church Street Boxing Gym, lower Manhattan):

```
LIVE TRADING ESPORTS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════
8 players                Three 30-min rounds
$25,000 paper money      crypto / Pengu memecoin
each                     weakest half eliminated
                         per round
Audience                 Prize: $10,000
predicts                 + ornate Japanese katana
Drones overhead          Play-by-play commentators
```

Hedge funds optimise **high return / low vol**. Single-elimination trading streamers optimise **maximum vol** — the product is entertainment, not capital allocation.

### Fund-formation fees — ILPA pushes for a cap

Two ways to think about a PE fund:

```
MODEL 1                              MODEL 2
The fund is a business               The sponsor is the business
owned by LPs.                        The fund is just a pool of capital.
Sponsor provides services.           Sponsor pays its own costs.
LPs pay all expenses.                LPs pay fees, that's it.
```

Hedge funds: traditionally Model 2 (2-and-20, manager pays own costs). **Modern multi-strategy pod shops** flipped to Model 1 — billing salaries, bonuses, jets, office art to LPs.

Private equity went the opposite direction. **3 guys with a Rolodex** couldn't afford anything → LPs paid everything. PE firms are now trillion-AUM institutions, and _"it feels churlish to bill clients for dinner"_ — but they still do.

**The new ILPA ask** (Institutional Limited Partners Association):

> _"I challenge you to find an industry where the client pays the cost of legal counsel negotiating against them."_ — Jennifer Choi, ILPA CEO

Their proposal: **cap fund-formation legal / admin / compliance costs at the lower of $10M or 0.05% of target fund size.**

Levine takes up the challenge — **public-company sell-side M&A**:

```
THE PUBLIC-M&A COUNTEREXAMPLE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Target hires lawyers who negotiate against the buyer.
        │
        ▼
Buyer agrees to a fixed price.
        │
        ▼
Target pays its lawyers out of the closing proceeds.
        │
        ▼
Net effect: BUYER pays for TARGET's lawyers.
            Target's lawyers have no cost discipline.
            (Sound familiar?)
```

Same structural pattern: the side that nominally hires the lawyer is not the side that economically bears the cost.
